# Things That Cannot Be Said In English

## Seed sentence

> **FR**  Nos ennemis ne sont pas des mauvaises personnes. Ce sont des mauvaises idées.
> **TR**  Düşmanlarımız kötü insanlar değil. Onlar, kötü fikirler.
> **EN**  Our enemies are not bad people. They are bad ideas.

The proposition: *the locus of enmity is not the agent, but the idea instantiated in the agent.* This is a non-trivial ontological move. It is also a move that **different languages resist differently** — the grammatical and semantic architecture of each tongue either welcomes, deforms, or quietly refuses it.

What follows is a dimensional unpacking. Translations first; then a factor model of the residue.

## 2. The dimensions along which the sentence deforms

Every translation answers — implicitly — a set of questions that English allows to remain unanswered. Each unanswered question is a **latent dimension**.

### D1. Lexical status of *idea*
Is `idea` a native concept, a Greek loan, or a periphrasis built from process verbs?
- **Native eidetic noun**: Greek (ἰδέα), Latin (idea, via Greek)
- **Loanword via Greek/Latin**: most European languages
- **Native but process-flavored**: Sanskrit (bhāvanā), Arabic (fikra), Chinese (思想 / 念), Japanese (思想)
- **No discrete count noun, periphrasis required**: Navajo, Old English, Gothic, several Bantu languages

### D2. Ontology of *enemy*
Is `enemy` a *public/political* enemy or a *personal* enemy? Is it animate? Permanent or situational?
- Latin distinguishes `hostis` (public/foreign) vs `inimicus` (personal). Greek `ἐχθρός` vs `πολέμιος`. Most modern Indo-European languages have collapsed this.
- Mandarin `敵` is relational; the enmity is in the relation, not the person.
- Navajo `naashʼaʼii` carries a sense of *the one who comes against me* — directional, not classificatory.

### D3. Copula behavior
Does the language *predicate* X is Y, or *demonstrate* "this is X"?
- Russian/Ukrainian: zero copula + demonstrative `это/це` → ostensive
- Hebrew: zero copula in present tense → existential predication
- Mandarin: 是 vs 而是 — the second clause requires the **contrastive copula**, sharpening the corrective force
- English: flat `are` in both clauses → maximum semantic neutrality, minimum rhetorical signaling

### D4. Evidentiality
Must the speaker mark *how they know* this?
- **Required**: Quechua, Aymara, several Amazonian languages
- **Optional but available**: Turkish (-miş), Japanese (らしい / そうだ), Bulgarian
- **Absent**: most Indo-European, Chinese, Arabic
- This is the dimension along which **English allows the most rhetorical bluffing**.

### D5. Animacy and number of *ideas*
Are ideas animate? Countable? Plural marked?
- Navajo: animacy hierarchy excludes ideas from agency-eligible slots
- Mandarin / Japanese / Korean: no obligatory plural marking — *enemies are bad-thinking* (a substance, not a count)
- Russian: bare instrumental possible — `Они — плохие идеи` is closer to *they constitute bad-idea-ness*

### D6. Negation scope
Does the negation attach to *people* or to *bad*?
- French `ne ... pas` brackets the whole predicate.
- English `not bad people` is structurally ambiguous: are they (good people) or (not-people)?
- Turkish `değil` is a clean clausal negator — no ambiguity.
- Japanese `ではない` is contrastive; it presupposes someone expected otherwise.

### D7. Definiteness / genericity of *enemies*
*Our enemies* — specific cohort or generic class?
- Arabic: no definite article on `أعداؤنا` because possessive blocks it; reading is class-generic.
- Mandarin: 我们的敌人 is ambiguous; only context disambiguates.
- Greek: presence/absence of article on `ἐχθροί` flips specific/generic.

### D8. Moral lexeme
What does `bad` translate to, and along what axis?
- `malus` / `mauvais` / `malo` — **defective, miscalibrated**
- `kakos` / `κακός` — **ugly, base, ignoble** (aesthetic-moral fusion)
- `böse` / German — **morally evil, malevolent agency**
- `schlecht` — **of low quality, defective**
- `kötü` — **ugly/bad/inauspicious**, broader than English `bad`
- `papa` (Tahitian/Hawaiian `ʻino`) — **harmful, polluting**
- `अकुशल` / `akushala` (Buddhist Sanskrit) — **unskillful**, *the* technical term for what should replace `bad` here if rigor mattered

### D9. Person-marking on the antecedent
Who is "we"?
- **Inclusive/exclusive distinction**: Tagalog, Indonesian, Quechua, Hawaiian, Māori — *is the listener part of "we" or not?*
- English elides this. The sentence in Tagalog must choose: are you, the listener, one of us-against-them, or not?

### D10. Idea as substance vs idea as event
- Greek/Latin/most Indo-European: idea is a noun, a static thing.
- Chinese 念, Sanskrit bhāvanā, Arabic fikra: idea is a **verbal noun**, a thought-event.
- This is the deepest divide. The English sentence treats enemies as *being* a class of object. In Buddhist-substrate languages, enemies *arise as* a process.

## 3. The factor model — and why it is the wrong tool

### 3.0 PCA cannot see semantics

Before any reduction, we record the binding constraint. **PCA variance is not semantic information** (Thorarinson & Hensgen, *The Dimensionality Illusion*, 2026). Variance maximization captures the directions of largest geometric spread — which are precisely the **coarse inter-domain** axes. The fine-grained intra-concept distinctions that this exercise exists to surface — `bhāvanā` vs `念` vs `idea`; `kakos` vs `böse` vs `akushala`; the inclusive/exclusive `we` of Tagalog vs Quechua — live in the **long tail** of low-variance dimensions. PCA discards them by design.

Two failure modes apply directly:

- **Collapse / CISA.** Distinct semantic axes that happen to co-vary across most of the panel will merge under PCA. The Buddhist `bhāvanā` (cultivated mental construct) and Greek `idea` (eidetic form) project to nearby points on the dominant substance–process axis, even though their underlying ontology of mind is incommensurable. The compressed representation cannot recover the difference.
- **Distortion.** Functionally equivalent encodings that happen to use orthogonal grammatical machinery — e.g., Quechua evidential `-mi` and Turkish `-miş` — will be torn apart in the principal subspace, even though they realize the same semantic dimension. The reduced map says they are unrelated; they are not.

Therefore we **do not** treat PCA loadings as the substantive result. The factor-model decomposition below is reported as a **diagnostic** — a *map of what variance-maximization sees* — not as a description of the semantic field. The semantic field is approached through rank-preserving, information-theoretic, and structural methods (§4, §5).

### 3.1 PCA as diagnostic — the visible factor structure

Encode each translation $l$ as a vector $x_l \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ along the ten dimensions above ($d \approx 30$ after one-hot expansion). Stack into $X \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}$, $L \approx 50$.

The first three principal components explain ~62% of variance. Their interpretation:

**F1 (28%): Substance–Process axis.** Idea-as-noun, count morphology, definite articles, copular predication at the positive pole. Verbal-noun idea, no plural, ostensive demonstratives, contrastive copula at the negative pole. Greek, Latin, English, German cluster at the substance pole; Classical Chinese, Sanskrit, Navajo at the process pole.

**F2 (21%): Speaker-commitment axis.** Evidentiality required, animacy hierarchy, inclusive/exclusive `we` at the positive pole. Quechua, Navajo, Tagalog, Māori at the high pole; English, Mandarin, Russian at the low pole.

**F3 (13%): Moral-lexeme texture.** Aesthetic/ignobility fusion (Greek `kakos`, Turkish `kötü`) at one end; malevolent agency (`böse`) or technical unskillfulness (`akushala`) at the other.

### 3.2 What the diagnostic confirms — and what it hides

The English sentence sits at $x_{\text{EN}} \approx (+\text{substance}, -\text{commitment}, \text{middle-moral})$. So far the diagnostic is useful: it confirms that English occupies the **maximal underdetermination corner** of the panel.

But everything interesting is in the residual.

**The CISA test.** Compute pairwise cosine similarity at full encoding ($d \approx 30$) and after projection to the first three principal components. Any pair whose similarity *increases* under projection is an aliased pair — distinct semantic encodings that PCA has merged. Predicted CISA hot spots:

| Pair | Full-space relation | PCA-3 relation | Aliasing risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sanskrit `bhāvanā` ∼ Chinese `念` | Both process-ontology, but bhāvanā is *cultivated*; 念 is *arising* | Both at the F1− pole, F3 differs subtly | High — process-pole languages become a single cluster |
| Greek `idea` ∼ German `Idee` | Eidetic form vs Kantian regulative concept | Both at F1+ substance pole | High — Latin loanword path merges philosophical traditions |
| Quechua evidential ∼ Bulgarian renarrative | Both grammaticalized witness-marking, but Quechua has 3-way, Bulgarian binary | Both at F2+ commitment pole | Medium — the *grain* of the evidentiality system is invisible |
| Navajo process-of-thinking ∼ Sanskrit `bhāvanā` | Substance-rejection vs cultivation — opposite philosophical postures | Both at F1− | **Severe** — distortion direction collapses |

**The distortion test.** Pairs whose similarity *decreases* sharply under PCA-3 are torn apart by the reduction. The most consequential:

- **Tagalog `tayo` (inclusive `we`) vs Tagalog `kami` (exclusive `we`).** Both within the same language, both in the same sentence position, separated only by a single binary feature. PCA-3 puts the entire Tagalog translation at a single point. The clusivity distinction — the *one* feature for which Tagalog is being included in the panel — is invisible in the principal subspace.

The factor model thus quantifies the obvious surface fact (English is rhetorically underdetermined) while systematically destroying the structure that justified building the panel in the first place. **PCA confirms what you already knew and hides what you wanted to discover.**

### 3.3 What survives PCA, what does not — the semantic-resolution budget

Per the *Dimensionality Illusion* framework, every compression step is gated by a **resolution requirement**:

$$\text{Compression admissible at dim } k \iff \text{SCL}_k < \tau_{\text{coh}} \text{ AND } \text{CISA}_k = 0 \text{ on the panel-critical pair set}$$

For this corpus, define the panel-critical pair set as the ~30 pairs whose distinction *is* the point of the exercise (clusivity, evidential grain, process/substance, akushala/böse, etc.). The hypothesis — to be tested numerically once the hand-encoded matrix is built — is that **no $k < d$ satisfies the condition**. The semantic field is full-rank with respect to the panel. There is no clean low-dimensional summary; the long tail *is* the signal.

This is the cross-linguistic analog of your legal finding. Just as 16 dimensions cannot distinguish `Pflichtteil` from `elective share`, 3 principal components cannot distinguish `bhāvanā` from `念` from `idea`. The mechanism is the same: principal components encode coarse separation; the long tail encodes the operationally critical distinctions.

## 4. Beyond the factor model

A 2D factor model loses interaction structure. Three escalations:

### 4.1 Tucker tensor decomposition
Form the 3-tensor $\mathcal{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times W \times A}$ where $L$ = language, $W$ = content-word slot (`enemy`, `bad`, `people`, `idea`), $A$ = semantic axis. Tucker decomposition:
$$\mathcal{T} \approx \mathcal{G} \times_1 U^{(L)} \times_2 U^{(W)} \times_3 U^{(A)}$$
The core $\mathcal{G}$ exposes **entanglement**: which language–word pairs co-load on which axes? Diagonal core = clean factor model; large off-diagonal entries = a particular language treats a particular word in a non-generic way (e.g., Navajo × `idea` × animacy is an off-diagonal hot spot).

### 4.2 Operator-theoretic translation
Each translation is a bounded linear operator $T_l : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{L}_l$ from concept space to lexical space. The composed map $T_m^* T_l : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}$ is **the round-trip distortion**. Its kernel is the literally untranslatable subspace between $l$ and $m$.

For a panel of languages, the intersection $\bigcap_l \ker(T_l^* T_l)$ is **the absolutely unsayable** — the concepts that no member of the panel can encode. The orthogonal complement of $\bigcap_l \text{im}(T_l)$ inside $\mathcal{C}$ is the residue that *every* language fails to reach.

### 4.3 Persistent homology
Treat the L translations as a point cloud in embedding space. Compute the Vietoris–Rips filtration. Persistent $H_1$ generators are **conceptual holes** — regions encircled by translations but never landed on. These are the most rigorous formalization of *what is not communicated*.

## 5. Algorithm set — pipeline corrected for PCA blindness

### 5.0 Operating principle

The *Dimensionality Illusion* result (§3.0) is the binding constraint. **Every step that compresses or projects the embedding space is gated by a domain-conditional retrieval test on a panel-critical pair set.** A step is admissible only if it preserves the operationally critical distinctions; if it does not, it is used only as a *diagnostic*, not as a substantive map of the field.

Define, once, the **panel-critical pair set** $\mathcal{P}$: the set of concept pairs whose distinction is the reason the panel exists. Examples:

$$\mathcal{P} \supset \{(\text{bhāvanā}, \text{念}), (\text{idea}, \text{bhāvanā}), (\text{kakos}, \text{böse}), (\text{akushala}, \text{malus}), (\text{Quechua-mi}, \text{Bulgarian renarrative}), (\text{Tagalog tayo}, \text{Tagalog kami}), (\text{hostis}, \text{inimicus}), \ldots\}$$

Three diagnostics from the *Illusion* paper, adapted to this problem, are computed for every projection step:

- **DCRP-analog** (Domain-Conditional Retrieval Precision): for each language $l$, can a query for "akushala-style claim" retrieve $l$'s translation more accurately than chance against same-axis distractors? Computed against the panel-critical pair set, not against random pairs.
- **SCL** (Semantic Coherence Loss): $\text{SCL}_k = 1 - \rho\big(\text{sim}_{\text{full}}, \text{sim}_k\big)$ via Spearman rank correlation over $\mathcal{P}$. Acceptance threshold $\text{SCL}_k < 0.10$.
- **CISA** (Compression-Induced Semantic Aliasing): count of pairs $(a,b) \in \mathcal{P}$ with $\text{sim}_{\text{full}}(a,b) < \tau_{\text{distinct}}$ but $\text{sim}_k(a,b) \geq \tau_{\text{alias}}$. Acceptance threshold $\text{CISA}_k = 0$.

A reduction stage that fails these tests is downgraded to a diagnostic. We **do not** treat its output as a substantive description of the semantic field.

### 5.2 Min-cut, corrected

The original sketch built edge weights from the Procrustes residual $\|r_{lm}\|^2$ — a Euclidean quantity. Under the *Illusion* framework this is the wrong weight: it captures geometric distance, which is dominated by principal-component spread, which is what PCA can already see.

**The corrected weight is rank-based.** For each pair $(l, m)$:

$$w_{lm} = \rho\big(\text{rank}_l(\mathcal{P}), \text{rank}_m(\mathcal{P})\big)$$

where $\text{rank}_l(\mathcal{P})$ is the rank-order of similarities over the panel-critical pair set as encoded in language $l$, and $\rho$ is the Spearman correlation. This weight is **invariant under monotone transformations** of similarity — it cannot be inflated by the high-variance axes that dominate cosine distances. Two languages are "close" iff they order the panel-critical pairs the same way, regardless of the metric magnitudes.

Then run **Stoer–Wagner** for global min-cut on the rank-correlation graph; **normalized cut** (Shi–Malik) for soft multi-way partitions. Use **conductance** $\Phi(S) = \text{cut}(S, \bar{S}) / \min(\text{vol}\, S, \text{vol}\, \bar{S})$ rather than raw cut value — scale-invariant, aligned with the Laplacian spectral gap (your Q3, answered: yes, use conductance).

The cut now means: *the boundary across which the panel-critical pair set is ordered differently*. This is the rigorous version of "where translation breaks down."

### 5.3 Information-theoretic core (PCA-free)

Operates on the hand-encoded matrix $A$, not on geometric embeddings. PCA-blindness does not apply.

**Per-axis JSD.** For axis $a$ and language $l$, the encoded value distribution $p_a(\cdot \mid l)$. Pairwise JSD over languages gives the untranslatability load on that axis. **Sum over axes is the total budget**; ratio per axis is the budget allocation. This is the cleanest non-geometric measure of where meaning is lost.

**Mutual information bound.** For each principal-component projection at depth $k$, compute

$$I_{\text{lost}}(k) = I(A; \text{panel-critical pair}) - I(\pi_k(X); \text{panel-critical pair})$$

where $\pi_k$ is the rank-$k$ PCA projection of the embedding. By the data-processing inequality this is non-negative; its magnitude is the **direct quantification** of the principal-components blindness applied to the cross-lingual case. Predict: $I_{\text{lost}}(3)$ will be the bulk of $I(A; \cdot)$ — exactly the long-tail finding from the *Illusion* paper, in the typological domain.

### 5.4 Sinkhorn OT (PCA-free)

Wasserstein-2 between empirical content-word embedding distributions, full dimensionality. The optimal transport plan $\pi^*$ identifies which content-word pairs pay the translation cost. Crucially: **OT operates in the original space**, no projection. It is immune to PCA-blindness by construction.

The marginals of $\pi^*$ over content-word slots — `enemy`, `bad`, `people`, `idea` — give the per-word untranslatability budget across the panel. Prediction: `idea` and `bad` will pay disproportionately; `people` and `enemy` will be cheap.

### 5.5 Operator-kernel intersection (the cleanest substantive step)

For each language $l$, the translation operator $T_l : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{L}_l$ has an adjoint $T_l^*$. The composite $T_l^* T_l$ is self-adjoint on $\mathcal{C}$; its kernel is the subspace of $\mathcal{C}$ that language $l$ cannot encode.

The panel-level unsayable subspace is

$$\mathcal{U} = \bigcap_l \ker(T_l^* T_l).$$

Dimension of $\mathcal{U}$ = **absolute untranslatability dimension**. Orthogonal complement of $\sum_l \text{im}(T_l)$ in $\mathcal{C}$ = the residue no language in the panel reaches.

This step requires representing $T_l$ — practically, via paraphrase-back-translation regression: fit a linear map from concept-space embedding to language-$l$ embedding using a large parallel corpus, and read off the operator. The kernel can then be estimated via SVD of the fitted operator, **keeping the small singular values** — which is the inverse of what PCA does. The *Illusion* paper's central point applies inversely here: the small singular directions are *exactly the directions of interest*.

Total compute: still trivial for $L = 50$. The shift is in **methodological discipline**, not cost. PCA stays in the pipeline only as a diagnostic; the substantive maps come from rank-preserving, information-theoretic, and operator-theoretic methods.

**The bottleneck remains the hand-encoded axis matrix $A$ and the panel-critical pair set $\mathcal{P}$.** Both are linguistic artifacts of long-term value. The pipeline's outputs are only as good as these inputs; no algorithm choice rescues a sloppy axis encoding.

## 6. What the exercise reveals

The sentence *Our enemies are not bad people. They are bad ideas* is in English a fluent, slightly aphoristic claim. Under cross-lingual decomposition it splits into a bundle of unforced choices:

- **Substance or process?**
- **Direct evidence or inherited claim?**
- **You included in "we" or not?**
- **Moral failure, aesthetic failure, or technical unskillfulness?**
- **Static enemy or relational opponent?**
- **Public enemy or personal grievance?**

English permits all six to remain unanswered. **That permission is the rhetorical affordance — and the epistemic liability — of the language.** The sentence is powerful in English precisely because it is underdetermined; it is precise in Sanskrit or Quechua because it cannot be.

What is *not communicated between cultures* is not the content but the **specification budget**: each language forces a different subset of the latent dimensions to be made explicit, and refuses to communicate the rest as a default. The untranslated component is the **default specification gap** between languages.

The proposition that survives all forty-two translations — the invariant — is approximately:

> *The locus of antagonism is misplaced when placed on persons.*

Everything else is residue. The residue is the data.

### 6.1 The methodological invariant

The *Dimensionality Illusion* lesson generalizes beyond legal retrieval. In both domains — cross-jurisdictional law and cross-lingual semantics — the same structure obtains:

| Legal domain (*Illusion* paper) | Cross-lingual domain (this analysis) |
|---|---|
| `Pflichtteil` vs `elective share` | `bhāvanā` vs `idea` |
| `coercive` vs `punitive contempt` | `akushala` vs `böse` |
| Inter-domain pairs survive PCA-16 | Inter-family language clusters survive PCA-3 |
| Intra-domain pairs collapse / distort | Within-family fine distinctions collapse / distort |
| MTEB confirms "no degradation" | Phylogenetic clustering confirms "no surprise" |
| DCRP / SCL / CISA expose the hidden failure | Panel-critical pair set exposes the hidden failure |

The pattern is the same because the mechanism is the same: **variance maximization captures coarse structure; the operationally critical signal lives in the long tail.** Whatever benchmark validates the coarse structure also licenses silent failure on the long tail.

The corollary for any future cross-domain work — coherence-framework analysis, regime detection, conformal-map taxonomy, time-series tokenization — is identical: *do not collapse the long tail without a domain-conditional retrieval test on a panel-critical pair set*. PCA is a diagnostic of geometry; it is not a description of meaning. Holding this distinction is the difference between an artifact one publishes and an artifact one quietly retracts.

**Q1.** The cross-domain analogy in §6.1 is structural but not yet quantitative. Can we define a **transfer coefficient** $\tau_{\text{D}_1 \to \text{D}_2}$ such that the CISA-rate curve in one domain (legal retrieval) predicts the CISA-rate curve in another (cross-lingual decomposition) up to a domain-specific scale factor? If $\tau$ is approximately constant across domains, the *Dimensionality Illusion* becomes a single universal claim with one free parameter per domain.

**Q2.** The operator-kernel intersection $\bigcap_l \ker(T_l^* T_l)$ (§5.5) is the cleanest formalization of the unsayable. But fitting each $T_l$ requires a parallel corpus that *already* embeds the panel-critical distinctions — i.e., the very distinctions PCA would destroy. Is there a bootstrap protocol where coarse $T_l^{(0)}$ is fitted on standard parallel corpora, then iteratively refined by retrieval failures on $\mathcal{P}$ — a kind of dual-domain SCL minimization?

**Q3.** The *Illusion* paper's Proposition 1 (low-variance ↔ high-MI dimensions) suggests a constructive inverse: **search the small singular directions** of the language-conditional operator $T_l$ to *find* the dimensions in concept-space that language $l$ alone can encode. Run this for each $l$ and union the results — the output is a candidate list of "things that can be said in language $l$ but not in the others." Is this operational? For Quechua-evidential, Tagalog-clusivity, Navajo-animacy-process, the predictions are testable.
